Built for Mississauga

Mississauga properties, Mississauga expertise

  • 🔧

    Stack effect in the City Centre and Airport Corporate Centre towers

    Mississauga has real high-rise stock now — the office towers around Robert Speck Parkway and Matheson Boulevard, and the residential towers ringing Square One. Tall buildings behave like chimneys in an Ontario winter: warm air rises up the stairs and elevator shafts and cold air is drawn in low, so lower-level stair doors become heavy to pull open while upper-floor doors get sucked shut hard enough to bang. Cranking every closer to maximum spring power is the wrong answer, because it makes the doors unusable for anyone with limited strength. We size the closer to the opening, use the backcheck and delayed-action valves properly, and treat pressure problems as pressure problems rather than hardware problems.

  • 🔧

    High-cycle doors in the Dixie-Britannia and Heartland industrial belt

    Mississauga holds one of the largest concentrations of industrial floor space in the country, and the personnel doors beside those dock levellers are the hardest-worked doors we see. They get flung open into the block wall, shouldered through with a hand truck, and clipped by a forklift corner sooner or later. What comes back to us is a torn-out closer shoe, a bent arm, a stripped fastener pattern in a hollow metal door with no reinforcement behind it, and a backcheck that gave up long ago. We fix the mounting as well as the closer — sex bolts through the door, proper backing plates, and an arm and mounting choice that suits the traffic instead of whatever was cheapest at build time.

  • 🔧

    Fire separations in condo and apartment common areas

    Condo corridors, stairwells, garbage chute rooms, locker areas and parking garage vestibules are almost all fire separations, and the doors in them are required to close and latch on their own. Ontario Fire Code Division B, Section 2.2.3 is explicit: doors in fire separations may not be wedged, blocked or otherwise held open except by a device that releases on alarm, and self-closing devices have to be maintained in operating condition. The 2026 amendments tightened the routine checks on those doors to a monthly basis. We work with Mississauga property managers and condo boards on exactly this — correcting the doors that fail the check and noting what was done so it is defensible at the next inspection.

  • 🔧

    Hydraulic closers stiffening in a Peel winter, then racing in a humid July

    A door closer is a hydraulic device, and hydraulic fluid gets thicker as it gets colder. The same closer that felt right in September becomes slow and heavy in January, stalls a few inches short of the strike, and leaves a fire door standing unlatched — or leaves an entrance hanging open with the heat pouring out and the vestibule icing up. Staff respond by winding the spring tension up, which is exactly what makes the door too fast and too hard to open once Mississauga returns to a humid thirty-degree August. Doors set for real Ontario conditions get their sweep, latch and backcheck valves adjusted with the season, not the spring power cranked once and forgotten.

  • 🔧

    Road salt, grit and ice at the threshold

    Brine and grit off the 401, 403, 410 and the QEW get tracked straight into Mississauga entrances all winter. That slurry packs under door bottoms and sweeps, builds a ridge of refrozen slush at the threshold, and stops a correctly adjusted door a centimetre short of latching — which reads as a closer fault and usually is not. It is worse on floor closers, where meltwater drains into the cement case and quietly corrodes the spindle and body until the door starts to drop. We check thresholds, sweeps and drop seals as part of any closer call, and specify cased-in floor closers with salt exposure in mind rather than replacing the same unit every few years.

  • 🔧

    Glass entrances on the plaza and power-centre strips

    Aluminum entrances with full glass lites are the default across the Hurontario, Dundas and Lakeshore plazas, the Heartland power centre and the Erin Mills and Square One retail blocks. Those doors are unforgiving of a bad closer: with no working backcheck the door swings hard into the stop or an adjacent handrail, and in mid-winter that impact lands on glass already under thermal stress from a cold outer face and a heated lobby. Add a slam-shut return and you are cracking lites, splitting stiles and loosening corner joints. We set backcheck and latch speed on entrance doors as a matter of course, because it is far cheaper than replacing tempered glass in February.

Mississauga FAQs

Your questions, answered

Do you only work on commercial buildings in Mississauga?

Commercial and multi-residential is the bulk of it — office towers, plazas, industrial units, schools, clinics and condo common areas across Peel. We also handle the residential side of a condo or apartment building, and heavy residential entrances that need a proper closer rather than a hardware-store spring hinge.

Are you licensed and insured?

Ontario has no provincial trade licence for door hardware or door closer work, so nobody in this trade holds one — be wary of anyone who says otherwise. What we do carry is full liability insurance and WSIB coverage, with certificates available on request, plus a written workmanship guarantee on the work we do.

How quickly can you attend a fire door that will not latch?

That is a priority call. Ontario Fire Code requires self-closing devices on fire separations to be kept in operating condition and fire doors never to be wedged open, so a door that will not latch gets a same-day or after-hours response across Mississauga and the surrounding Peel area.

Why do our closers get so much worse in winter?

Hydraulic fluid thickens as it gets colder, so a closer that felt right in September turns slow and heavy in January and often stalls short of the latch. Buildings also move slightly as they cool and the latch drifts off the strike. Both are adjustable, and doors set for real Ontario conditions get a seasonal adjustment rather than a permanent spring increase.

How do you price closer and exit hardware work?

Every job is quoted after a free, no-obligation look at the openings, then given to you as one written fixed-scope estimate. What you approve is what you pay, with no surprise add-ons — and no numbers guessed over the phone before anyone has seen the doors.

All of it, plus the surrounding Peel area — City Centre, Cooksville, Port Credit, Streetsville, Erin Mills, Meadowvale, Malton, Applewood, Dixie, Sheridan Park, Churchill Meadows and Clarkson, including the Airport Corporate Centre and Heartland business parks.

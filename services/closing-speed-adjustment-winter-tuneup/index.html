This is the most useful and most misunderstood thing we do. A closer has separate valves for sweep speed (the main part of the swing), latch speed (the last few inches into the strike) and backcheck (the cushion that stops the door being flung into the wall), plus spring power and sometimes delayed action. Turning them at random is how a door ends up slamming, stalling short of the latch, or so heavy that people cannot open it — and in a Mississauga winter, cold thickened fluid pushes every one of those settings out of range on its own.

A seasonal tune-up gets the door doing what it should at the temperature it actually lives in: full closure with a positive latch when it is minus twenty, no slam when the vestibule warms up, and an opening force that stays within reach. It matters for accessibility too — the Ontario Building Code limits closers on a barrier-free path of travel to an opening force of no more than 38 N at exterior doors and 22 N at interior doors, with an interior closing period of at least three seconds from seventy degrees open, so simply winding the spring up to force a latch is not a legitimate fix. We tune closers building by building, usually as a scheduled autumn pass through a property manager's Mississauga portfolio before the first hard freeze.

FAQs

Closing Speed Adjustment & Winter Tune-Ups — your questions

Why does our door stop latching every winter?

Hydraulic fluid thickens as it cools, so the closer moves the door more slowly than it did in September and it runs out of momentum before the latch engages. Add wind on an exposed frontage and it stops short every time. This is normal and predictable, and it is corrected by adjusting for the season rather than by fitting a stronger spring.

Can we not just turn the spring tension up and be done with it?

That is the usual in-house fix, and it creates the next problem. A door heavy enough to beat a January draft is a door nobody can pull open, and on a barrier-free route it can push you past the opening force that route is allowed. We balance the settings instead, and correct whatever was making the door hard work in the first place.

Can you tune a whole property in one visit?

Yes, and it is far better value than individual call-outs. On a multi-building site across Peel we walk every opening, adjust what will adjust, flag the units that have lost fluid, verify that rated doors latch from a full open position, and hand back one written list of what was done and what still needs parts.

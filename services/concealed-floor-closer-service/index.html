Where the building did not want a closer on show — condo and office lobbies around City Centre, bank and clinic entrances, glass storefronts on the plaza strips — you get a concealed unit instead. Overhead-concealed closers live inside the frame head or the top rail of the door; floor closers sit in a cement case in the slab under the pivot. Both are far less forgiving than a surface closer: the door usually has to come off to reach them, the arms and track are specific to the unit, and a lot of Mississauga buildings are running units that went out of production a long time ago.

Floor closers are the ones that fail hardest here. Meltwater carrying road salt runs off boots straight into the case, and over enough winters it corrodes the spindle and body until the door starts to drop, drag on the threshold and stop latching. Overhead-concealed units usually fail through leaking bodies or a worn track and slide arm. We identify the unit, source or cross-reference the correct replacement, and handle the part everyone else avoids — pulling and re-hanging a heavy glass or aluminum door safely, resetting pivots and top centres, and getting the door plumb and square again. Where a discontinued floor closer cannot be matched, we will tell you honestly whether a retrofit unit or a properly specified overhead alternative is the better long-term answer.

FAQs

Concealed & Floor Closer Service — your questions

Our lobby door has no visible closer. Who fixes that?

We do. It will be either an overhead-concealed closer inside the frame head or door top rail, or a floor closer in a cement case under the pivot. Both need the door taken off to service, which is why they get neglected — but both are repairable or replaceable.

Why did our floor closer fail so quickly?

Winter. Salty meltwater drains off footwear into the floor case and corrodes the unit from the inside, which is why floor closers in Ontario entrances have a much harder life than the same unit indoors. Correct case drainage and a unit specified for that exposure make a real difference to how long the next one lasts.

Our concealed closer is a discontinued model. What are our options?

There is usually a current unit that cross-references to the same frame or case preparation, and that is our first choice because it avoids cutting anything. If nothing matches, we will lay out the honest options — a retrofit unit, adapting the preparation, or moving to a properly sized overhead closer — before you commit to anything.

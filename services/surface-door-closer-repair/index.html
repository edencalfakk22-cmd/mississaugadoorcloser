The surface-mounted rack-and-pinion closer is the workhorse of every Mississauga office, plaza, school and industrial unit, and it fails in a small number of predictable ways. It starts leaking oil down the door face and loses all control. The arm bends or the shoe tears out of a hollow metal door that never had proper reinforcement. The body is a size too small for a wide, heavy or wind-exposed opening, so it has been wound up to maximum just to make the door latch. Or it is simply thirty years old and out of production, and the last person to touch it turned all three valves at random.

We diagnose the opening before we quote hardware. That means checking the door and frame for square, the hinges or pivots for wear, and the actual spring size the opening calls for — then choosing between regular arm, top jamb and parallel arm mounting to suit the swing, the traffic and the wall condition. A leaking closer body is a sealed unit and cannot be topped up, so it gets replaced, but a great many closers we are called to simply need correct mounting, a straight arm and the sweep, latch and backcheck set properly. Most Mississauga calls finish in one visit because common closer sizes, arms and brackets ride on the truck.

FAQs

Surface Door Closer Repair & Replacement — your questions

We have replaced the same dock-office closer twice in two years. What are we doing wrong?

Almost always sizing, mounting, or both. A door beside a loading dock can cycle several hundred times a shift, and a closer chosen off the door width alone will not survive that. Neither will an arm anchored into thin hollow metal with no reinforcement behind it. We size for cycle count and exposure, add backing where the prep has chewed out, and move to a heavier body when the opening warrants it.

There is oil running down the door under the closer. Can it be topped up?

No — the body is a sealed hydraulic unit with no fill port, so once the fluid is out it is out. Screwing the valves down on a dry closer only pushes the last of it onto the floor. The unit has to come off and be replaced, and on a rated door that is urgent rather than cosmetic.

Can you match new closers to the ones already through the building?

Usually, and it is worth doing. A building running one closer family is far cheaper to maintain because arms, brackets and covers interchange and a technician arrives with the right parts. Tell us the make on an existing unit, or send a photo of the cover, and we will confirm what is still available. Where a model has been discontinued we say so and propose the closest current equivalent that fits the same prep.

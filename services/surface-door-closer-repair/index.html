The surface-mounted rack-and-pinion closer is the workhorse of every Mississauga office, plaza, school and industrial unit, and it fails in a small number of predictable ways. It starts leaking oil down the door face and loses all control. The arm bends or the shoe tears out of a hollow metal door that never had proper reinforcement. The body is a size too small for a wide, heavy or wind-exposed opening, so it has been wound up to maximum just to make the door latch. Or it is simply thirty years old and out of production, and the last person to touch it turned all three valves at random.

We diagnose the opening before we quote hardware. That means checking the door and frame for square, the hinges or pivots for wear, and the actual spring size the opening calls for — then choosing between regular arm, top jamb and parallel arm mounting to suit the swing, the traffic and the wall condition. A leaking closer body is a sealed unit and cannot be topped up, so it gets replaced, but a great many closers we are called to simply need correct mounting, a straight arm and the sweep, latch and backcheck set properly. Most Mississauga calls finish in one visit because common closer sizes, arms and brackets ride on the truck.

FAQs

Surface Door Closer Repair & Replacement — your questions

My door closer is leaking oil — can it be refilled?

No. A modern closer is a sealed hydraulic unit with no fill port, so once the fluid is out the body has to be replaced. A leaking closer either slams the door hard enough to split the frame or stalls short of the latch, and on a fire door that is a compliance problem as well as a nuisance.

The door slams. Can you just turn it down?

Often yes — sweep and latch speed are separate valves and most slamming doors are simply mis-adjusted or have no working backcheck. If the body is leaking or the internals are worn, no amount of adjusting will hold, and we will tell you that on site rather than charging for an adjustment that lasts a week.

The closer keeps tearing out of our warehouse door — why?

Almost always a mounting problem, not a closer problem. A hollow metal door in a high-traffic Mississauga industrial unit needs proper backing behind the shoe and through-bolts rather than sheet-metal screws, and the arm has to suit the way the door is actually used. We fix the mounting when we fit the closer, which is why the second one tends to stay put.

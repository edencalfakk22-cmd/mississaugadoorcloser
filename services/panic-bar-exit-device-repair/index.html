An exit device has one job that cannot be negotiated: a person leaning on it in the dark has to be outside in one motion. When a panic bar in a Mississauga plaza, warehouse or assembly space starts needing a shove, a lift or a second push, it has stopped being exit hardware. We service the three families you actually find here — rim devices with a surface strike, mortise devices tied into the lock case, and surface or concealed vertical rod devices on pairs and on doors that need top and bottom latching.

Vertical rod devices give us most of our call volume, and Mississauga's high-cycle industrial and retail doors are why. The bottom rod and latch sit right where road salt, grit and meltwater collect all winter, so they seize, the latch stops retracting fully, and the door starts dragging or refusing to release. Others fail because the door and frame moved out of square and the top latch no longer finds its strike. We rebuild or replace the device, free and re-set the rods, correct the strikes, and check that any dogging function and electric latch retraction still behave. We also look at what the door is doing overall, because an exit device fighting a badly set closer will fail again.

FAQs

Panic Bars & Exit Device Repair — your questions

Our panic bar needs two pushes to open. How urgent is that?

Treat it as urgent. A door on an exit route has to release in a single motion, and one that needs a second push or a lift is a life-safety issue rather than a maintenance ticket. Call us for priority dispatch, and never chain, padlock or block the door as a stopgap.

Can you repair the device or does the whole thing get replaced?

Most exit devices are repairable — end caps, latches, rods, strikes, dogging assemblies and trim are all serviceable parts. We replace the full device when the chassis itself is worn out or the unit has been out of production long enough that parts no longer exist, and we say which it is on site.

Why does our bottom vertical rod jam every winter?

Because the bottom latch sits exactly where salt, grit and refrozen slush collect on a Mississauga entrance. The abrasive slurry works into the latch and rod and seizes it. Cleaning, freeing and correctly adjusting the rods — plus sorting out the threshold and sweep — is what keeps it working through the season.

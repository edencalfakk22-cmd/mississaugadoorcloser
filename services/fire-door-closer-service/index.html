A fire door only works if it closes and latches by itself, without anyone helping it. Ontario Fire Code Division B, Section 2.2.3 requires that doors in fire separations be kept closed or held open only by a device that releases on alarm, that self-closing devices be maintained in operating condition, and that the opening be kept clear of anything that interferes with the door swinging shut. Fire Door Closers & Ontario Fire Code Compliance — your questions

Can we prop our fire door open until it gets fixed?

No. Wedging or blocking a fire door defeats the separation it exists to create and is one of the most common findings written up in Ontario buildings. If traffic flow is the real problem, the compliant answer is an electromagnetic hold-open tied to the fire alarm, which releases the door automatically on signal.

Our fire door closes but does not quite latch. Is that a failure?

Yes. A fire door has to positively latch on its own — a door resting against the stop will be pushed open by fire pressure. It is usually a latch speed set too low, a worn closer, or a strike that no longer lines up after the frame shifted, and all three are correctable without replacing the door.

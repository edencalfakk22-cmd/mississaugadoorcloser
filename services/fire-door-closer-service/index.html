A fire door only works if it closes and latches by itself, without anyone helping it. Ontario Fire Code Division B, Section 2.2.3 requires that doors in fire separations be kept closed or held open only by a device that releases on alarm, that self-closing devices be maintained in operating condition, and that the opening be kept clear of anything that interferes with the door swinging shut. FAQs

Fire Door Closers & Ontario Fire Code Compliance — your questions

Can we prop the fire door open until it gets fixed?

No. Wedging or chaining a fire door defeats the separation it exists to create, and it is one of the easiest items for an inspector to write up. If the door genuinely needs to stand open for traffic, the compliant answer is a hold-open released by the fire alarm, which we can fit to most rated openings.

Our fire door closes but does not quite latch. Is that a failure?

Treat it as one. A door resting against the strike is an open door as far as smoke is concerned. The requirement is that the closure is kept in operating condition — closing and latching by itself, from any open position, with nobody helping. It usually corrects with latch-speed adjustment, a properly sized closer or a strike repair, not a new door.

Do you leave a record we can put in our fire safety plan file?

Yes. We test every self-closing device we touch, note which doors latched, which were corrected on the spot and which need parts, and hand that back in writing. Managers running several buildings across Peel tell us that list is what an inspector actually wants to see.

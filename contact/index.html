Where we work in Mississauga

From City Centre, Cooksville, Port Credit, Streetsville, Erin Mills, Meadowvale, Malton, Applewood, Dixie, Sheridan Park, Churchill Meadows and Clarkson, we cover every Mississauga neighbourhood — plus the surrounding Peel communities.

Stack effect in the City Centre and Airport Corporate Centre towers. Mississauga has real high-rise stock now — the office towers around Robert Speck Parkway and Matheson Boulevard, and the residential towers ringing Square One. Tall buildings behave like chimneys in an Ontario winter: warm air rises up the stairs and elevator shafts and cold air is drawn in low, so lower-level stair doors become heavy to pull open while upper-floor doors get sucked shut hard enough to bang. Cranking every closer to maximum spring power is the wrong answer, because it makes the doors unusable for anyone with limited strength. We size the closer to the opening, use the backcheck and delayed-action valves properly, and treat pressure problems as pressure problems rather than hardware problems.

High-cycle doors in the Dixie-Britannia and Heartland industrial belt. Mississauga holds one of the largest concentrations of industrial floor space in the country, and the personnel doors beside those dock levellers are the hardest-worked doors we see. They get flung open into the block wall, shouldered through with a hand truck, and clipped by a forklift corner sooner or later. What comes back to us is a torn-out closer shoe, a bent arm, a stripped fastener pattern in a hollow metal door with no reinforcement behind it, and a backcheck that gave up long ago. We fix the mounting as well as the closer — sex bolts through the door, proper backing plates, and an arm and mounting choice that suits the traffic instead of whatever was cheapest at build time.
